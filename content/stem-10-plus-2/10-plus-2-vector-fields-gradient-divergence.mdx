# 10+2 Vector Algebra: Dot, Cross Products & Gradient Fields

Vectors are mathematical entities possessing both magnitude and direction. In engineering physics and 3D computer graphics, vectors form the foundation of force analysis, ray tracing, and spatial transformations.

### 1. The Dot Product (Scalar Product)

The scalar product between two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ measures projection and alignment:

<MathFormulaBlock formula="\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}| |\vec{b}| \cos(\theta) = a_x b_x + a_y b_y + a_z b_z" label="Dot Product Formula" />

<InfoBox title="Orthogonality Condition" type="tip">
Two non-zero vectors are perpendicular if and only if their dot product equals zero ($\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$). This property is used constantly in game engine lighting shaders to calculate Lambertian diffuse reflection.
</InfoBox>

### 2. The Cross Product (Vector Product)

The vector product creates a vector perpendicular to both input vectors following the right-hand rule:

<MathFormulaBlock formula="\vec{a} \times \vec{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ a_x & a_y & a_z \\ b_x & b_y & b_z \end{vmatrix} = (a_y b_z - a_z b_y)\hat{i} - (a_x b_z - a_z b_x)\hat{j} + (a_x b_y - a_y b_x)\hat{k}" label="Cross Product Determinant" />
