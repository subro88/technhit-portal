## § 01 / DERIVATIVES — The Slope of an Instant

For a continuous real-valued function $f(x)$, the derivative $f'(x)$ at any given point is the exact slope of the tangent line that kisses the curve. Formally, it represents the limit of the secant difference quotient as the increment step $h$ shrinks to zero:

<MathFormulaBlock formula="f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x + h) - f(x)}{h}" label="First Principle Definition of Derivative" />

## § 02 / INTEGRALS — Accumulation Rectified

While differentiation determines the instantaneous rate of change, integration operates as its exact mathematical inverse: accumulating uncountably many infinitesimal slices into a finite, closed-form area beneath the curve.

<MathFormulaBlock formula="\int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x" label="Definite Integral as Limit of Riemann Sums" />

## § 03 / LIMITS — The Epsilon-Delta Contract

Every theorem in differential and integral calculus rests upon the bedrock of Cauchy and Weierstrass's rigorous epsilon-delta limit definition. A limit is a binding contractual promise: for any arbitrary output tolerance band $\varepsilon > 0$ demanded, there exists an input neighborhood $\delta > 0$ that keeps the function strictly bounded within that tolerance.

<MathFormulaBlock formula="\forall \varepsilon > 0,\ \exists \delta > 0 : 0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon" label="Weierstrass Epsilon-Delta Limit Definition" />
